Four months ago, I felt the world was full of ideas, full of things I could do, and I had big hopes. But four months later, I'm almost disheartened and very confused. I don't know what to do or where the path is.

First, let's set the tone for this little summary:

First, I feel like I'm on the brink of failure. But when I calm down, this is the truest side of entrepreneurship. I don't think I've failed yet, and I must keep trying.

Second, this article isn't really a summary of lessons learned. Because my understanding isn't that deep yet, I can only share what I've done and how I feel.

# A bit of background

I entered the internet industry in 2005 and the mobile internet in 2009, working for over 20 years. As an early designer, I was [involved in a company's journey from zero to public listing](https://diff.im/blog/2020/05/%E6%88%91%E5%8A%A0%E5%85%A5%E6%B5%81%E5%88%A9%E8%AF%B4%E7%9A%84%E6%95%85%E4%BA%8B-%E6%9D%A5%E8%87%AA%E6%B5%81%E5%88%A9%E8%AF%B4%E7%AC%AC-7-%E5%8F%B7%E5%91%98%E5%B7%A5/%E5%89%8D%E8%AE%BE%E8%AE%A1%E6%80%BB%E7%9B%91%E7%9A%84%E5%88%86%E4%BA%AB/), worked on countless projects, and built a certain economic foundation.

It's not an exaggeration to say that for the past 20 years, I've always had this urge inside me to do something of my own. But my timid nature held me back when conditions weren't ripe, and even when I had built a certain economic foundation, I still didn't dare to act.

But by the end of 2025, that desire to do something on my own slowly resurfaced. Indeed, it was AI that gave me the courage to go create something.

Before venturing out to start a one-person company, I did two things:

Researched the state of one-person company entrepreneurship: This research was all done through AI. Based on certain data, AI provided me with a report. It told me that typically, there might be no income at all in the first 4 to 6 months, and under such conditions, only about 1% to 3% survive. This research gave me a basic mental preparation.

Conducted personal financial analysis: With my current living and spending level in the US, my financial situation could support me tinkering for 3 to 5 years. This gave me a basic expectation of the entrepreneurial timeline.

[At the end of March, I resigned and began exploring.](https://diff.im/blog/2026/04/%E5%85%B3%E4%BA%8E%E5%BC%80%E5%A7%8B%E5%81%9A%E7%8B%AC%E7%AB%8B%E5%BC%80%E5%8F%91%E7%9A%84%E4%B8%80%E4%BA%9B%E6%83%B3%E6%B3%95/)

# Four months, four products

## [Bible 2460](https://bible2460.com/)

The first product is a Bible clock - Bible 2460 [https://bible2460.com/](https://bible2460.com/) is a practice product. I'm an Apple loyalist and have always wanted to make an independent iOS app. Also, as a Christian, I have my own needs for reading the Bible. So this app was mainly to fulfill a personal wish, almost completely without considering payment, purely for entertainment, and making it gives me peace of mind.

Currently, this product has about 600 users, all of whom are free users. There are about 5 to 10 scattered downloads per day, with some organic traffic. This might be the best "data performer" among all my products.

## [VibeCap](https://vibecap.dev/en/)

The second product is VibeCap [https://vibecap.dev/en/](https://vibecap.dev/en/), a screenshot application serving AI programming.

When doing AI Coding, especially for designers, you often need to use screenshots to communicate details and optimize adjustments with AI.

Apple’s built-in screenshot tool isn’t that convenient, whereas VibeCap is a very quick, lightweight screenshot tool:

1. You can use the shortcut Command + C to screenshot and copy, then paste directly with Command + V.
2. Supports regular automatic cleanup of images.

When I initially released this tool on the Mac App Store, someone surprisingly paid for it, buying a $29 lifetime version right away. This gave me a lot of hope, or rather, an illusion.

After updating a few versions, there’s basically no noise.

## [Cap4u](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cap4u-english-call-coach/id6769973855)

The third product is the heaviest one I've made—Cap4U [https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cap4u-english-call-coach/id6769973855](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/cap4u-english-call-coach/id6769973855) —also a very idealistic product.

I immigrated to the US in 2022. Because my English isn’t good enough, I faced many challenges in life. Many friends around me also have the need and desire to improve their English.

At the same time, in American life, making phone calls is a very practical issue—you can't understand English calls and don’t know what to say.

So this product mainly offers the following functions:

1. AI subtitle translation: Helps users first understand what the other person is saying.
2. Native language translation: Uses AI to translate the user’s native language into the corresponding English, letting users know how to express themselves.
3. Pre-practice: Allows users to prepare a custom exercise in advance for a specific matter.

I can’t pronounce it a failure yet since I haven’t done any formal promotion. But I think it's probably also a relatively difficult, slow-growing product. Because its nature is somewhat against human tendencies, requiring users to have a very strong motivation to improve their English.

## [RXlingo](https://rxlingo.com/)

The fourth product is called RXlingo [https://rxlingo.com/](https://rxlingo.com/), and this is quite an interesting product.

At the time, I was releasing Cap4U and submitting for App Store review. While taking a break and with nothing to do, a thought suddenly struck me to make such a browser plug-in.

This is actually a product that I made rather comfortably, relatively smoothly. The concept is simple, but users might not buy it.

Here, I need to thank a friend. Although we don't know each other, he is also an individual entrepreneur.

At that time, he suggested, "First post a month's worth of videos to see."

I was inspired by him and started making videos.

Currently, my product has about 100 installation users, but the income is still zero.

I previously did some Reddit promotion targeting Japanese and Korean users, the costs are still acceptable at about $0.4 to $0.5 per click. But according to my business model, the current conversion rate is definitely a losing proposition.

Additionally, I persistently recorded four or five videos, posting them on Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Kuaishou, and WeChat Video Account, also successfully attracted some fans and received some likes and collections. Although these figures might be negligible compared to big IPs, I think it's a pretty good start for me.

# Not-so-deep reflections

That’s a rough introduction to the products. Next, I'll share some insights and reflections.

## Stepping out of comfort zone, doing things you don't want to do

I actually learned this lesson seven or eight years ago: the things I don't want to do and find difficult are often the things I most need to do.

I'm an introverted person with a lot of insecurities. From 2015 to 2019 when I was leading a team, public speaking was something that drove me crazy and I couldn't face it. But later on, I broke through, not only standing in front of people relatively comfortably but even enjoying the experience.

Now that I'm running my own company, I have to step out of my comfort zone. Two things specifically:

Marketing and promotion: This requires me to appear on camera and record videos. This was something I really didn't want to do before. In previous video recordings, I often felt like my tongue was tied. But there's no choice; I have to do it myself.

Communicating with people: Talking to people of different ages around me — I can't just call them "users". Chatting about life, their feelings, introducing and recommending products. Even though I'm not very articulate, I have to keep doing it.

If I want to make a living as a one-person company, I have to step out of my comfort zone.

I don't feel like this is making things difficult for myself. Instead, I feel like through this one-person company venture, I'm making my character and personal qualities more complete.

## One-Person Company Entrepreneurship Requires Holistic Personal Development

Even with powerful tools like AI, which can even be considered teammates, starting a one-person company still demands a lot from an individual:

1. Financial and time management: You need to have control over your finances and manage your time well.
2. Family and balance: Especially if you have a family and children, even though you're heavily invested in entrepreneurship, you can't neglect your family and kids. Otherwise, a successful venture would be meaningless.
3. Physical and mental health: You need to take care of your body because if your health fails, you have nothing. This includes not only physical health but also mental health, needing a community for communication and exchange.

## Regrets About Not Starting Sooner

One regret I have is not establishing my social channels earlier.

Even though I had already been writing on my public account for ten years, I didn't deliberately increase my followers; I just wrote casually. After ten years, I only had 1500 followers. On platforms like Xiaohongshu, I was just a spectator. It wasn't until a month ago, in July, that I consciously started expanding my social channels.

Even though progress is slow, there's still some improvement:

1. Like with my public account, I spent some time, and the number of followers increased from 1500 to about 2500.
2. Also, a tweet I shared earlier brought me nearly 500 follows.

Why do this? Because the cost of distribution is just too high.

To give a simple example: For my Cap4U iOS App, when I tested App Ads, each click cost $2, but hardly resulted in any exposure. Even with clicks, there were no installs.

On the other hand, for RXlingo, I posted a series of videos on Xiaohongshu, and quite a few followers reached out, asking how to start experiencing it.

In the AI era, distribution is more important than development. This is already common knowledge, but you only gain such a deep understanding by going through it yourself.

## Communicate More with Family

When I started the "one-person company" venture, I had a serious conversation with my wife and kids (the eldest is 16, and the second is 11) about my ideas, explaining why I'm doing this. Actually, I wanted to share with the kids, so they understand my thoughts and have a basic awareness and concept of entrepreneurship.

Over the past four months, my wife, needless to say, and even the two kids are very concerned about my progress. I also honestly tell them that sometimes I get a little payment from users, but most of the time there's no income, and they worry about it. My son even asked me how much money we have. Of course, I can't tell him the exact number. A few days ago, when we were chatting, my son directly asked, "Why don't you get a job?"

I also told him that for me, finding a job might be the simplest solution right now, but it's not that easy to find a good one. Moreover, in the long run, I can't always work for others. That's not something of long-term value for me. So even without income now, I still need to keep exploring.

Through such communication and sharing, I feel that, even if I don't break through and need to go back to work, this experience is not just mine, but belongs to our whole family.

## Disconnect Between Faith and Entrepreneurship

At first, I was full of confidence, thinking I was unstoppable with AI. As a Christian, I felt no different from ordinary people in entrepreneurship, diving in and working relentlessly. I even felt that my development work didn't need faith, had no relation to faith, and was completely disconnected from it.

When I reached such a state, I was actually moving further from my faith and my relationship with God. The Bible says, "If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in them." I've always known this and often got reminded, but I couldn't live it out while in that state.

About four months in, I received some reminders, including one from my wife. She told me I needed to pray and entrust it all. Initially, I joked, saying, "Why pray when we have AI? I think AI can solve all these problems."

While it's true AI might solve technical problems, my mindset issues, confidence issues, and perspective issues, I believe AI can't solve. Whether my thinking is clear and awake is also not something AI can solve.

So by the fourth month, I kept recalling a Bible verse. It's what Peter and his companions said after Jesus' resurrection, when they went out fishing: "We've worked hard all night and haven't caught anything." That's exactly my current state — working hard day and night for four months but not getting much.

Where Peter's faith stood out was when Jesus told him to "put out into deep water, and let down the nets for a catch" (Luke 5:4), and Peter did it. Peter was a fisherman, a very professional one; Jesus was not an expert and arguably knew nothing about fishing. But for Peter, a professional, to set aside his knowledge and methods and listen to a non-expert’s guidance required immense faith.

So for me, how should I return to God? I also need to let go of my stubbornness, seeking God's guidance on what to do next. I try to avoid getting into "prosperity theology," but I can't deny that I do want positive feedback, I think I can pray for that.

But I think, for me, the core, the most important goal is not just success. In other words, if I don't succeed, I believe I can accept it. I hope more to reconnect with God through this process.

On August 17, it was my kid's first day of sixth grade. He attends a Christian school. At the opening ceremony, we listened to a pastor's message.

The pastor's words were simple, but one line, which I've heard many times before, really resonated with me this time. The pastor said he hopes the children will learn and grow in this school not just for themselves, but to glorify God; that the knowledge and skills they acquire are also to glorify God.

This made me reflect on myself: my learning, my knowledge, my abilities should also be for the glory of God.

## Finally, It's Not That I Gained Nothing

First, I figured out the basic concepts and processes of AI programming:

1. Managing and using GitHub.
2. How to build a harness, and how to write, reference, or create Skills yourself.
3. Doing automated testing.
4. Data point tracking with Mixpanel.
5. Launch campaigns.

I've also started doing some marketing and social channel promotion, accumulating some followers.

I've found some footing in creating content on Xiaohongshu, with each piece getting dozens to hundreds of saves and likes.

Lastly, my skin has gotten thicker. 😄
